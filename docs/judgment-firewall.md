# Judgment Firewall v1 — minimizing poor choices by design

Adopted 2026-10-13 on the operator' instruction. Companion to the Blindspot Protocol (docs/blindspot-protocol.md) and Tracker PM Standard v1 (docs/pm-standard.md). The Blindspot Protocol catalogs the errors; the Firewall is the architecture that keeps them from becoming decisions.

**Principle:** don't fix judgment — shrink the territory where raw judgment operates, and fortify what remains.

## The hierarchy — every decision is pushed down as far as it can go

1. **Eliminate.** A recurring decision with a known-good answer becomes a rule the system executes. It stops being a decision.
2. **Constrain.** Gates and checklists with required artifacts: no proceeding past a missing field, a stale figure, or an unnamed owner.
3. **Structure.** What survives gets the Blindspot Protocol formula — base-rate-anchored, evidence-quality-weighted, ranked.
4. **Challenge.** An adversary whose only job is to kill the decision, plus independent estimates locked before any senior voice speaks.
5. **Allow — logged and scored.** Only genuinely novel calls reach raw judgment, each with a pre-registered resolution date, so the judgment becomes data.

## Decision rights (tiers)

- **Tier 0 — mechanism decides:** recurring, reversible, known-good (filing, formatting, routine checks inside standing rules). No human or agent judgment call is recorded because none is made.
- **Tier 1 — agent decides, logged + scored:** reversible operational calls inside a project's declared appetite and metrics.
- **Tier 2 — adversary-gated:** consequential staffing verdicts, project continuation/kill, scope and appetite changes. Requires: locked independent estimates, a recorded adversary kill attempt, and a disconfirmation artifact. The decider is never the original proposer.
- **Tier 3 — the operator only:** spend, outside send, public post, deletion, hire, legal or financial commitment (the hard rails (§11 of the operating charter)). Non-delegable at any score; the formula may recommend, only he releases.

A decision record carries its tier and cannot resolve above it. Attempting to settle a Tier 2 question at Tier 1 is itself logged as a firewall breach.

## Error budgets for judgment

Each decision category (estimates, staffing verdicts, continuation calls, data classifications) carries a rolling error budget: the tolerated rate of resolved-poor outcomes over its window. When a category breaches budget, judgment in that category escalates one tier automatically and stays escalated until two consecutive clean review cycles restore it. Authority is a privilege calibration earns and data revokes. Budget state (remaining, burn rate, escalation status) is board-visible.

## Noise audits

Periodically, the same decision case is presented to multiple deciders independently (locked before reveal, anti-HIPPO). The spread is recorded. High variance means the decision type is unstructured — it is pushed down the hierarchy (structured, constrained, or mechanized) rather than the deciders being blamed. Noise is treated as a property of the process, measured like any other defect.

## Friction asymmetry

The correct path is the easy path: defaults pre-fill the compliant option; proceeding requires no extra writing. Deviating requires extra steps and a written reason that lands in the deviation log, which the weekly review reads. Willpower is not a control; architecture is.

## Feedback speed

Every Tier 1+ decision carries a resolution date at registration. Unresolved past-due decisions are unfinished work; their count is a standing alarm in the weekly review. A decision category whose resolutions chronically slip loses Tier 1 standing until its feedback loop closes on time.

## The floor (honest limit)

Novel situations have no reference class; there, judgment is unavoidable. The Firewall's job at the floor: prefer the reversible option, impose a cooling-off period on irreversible Tier 2 calls, require two keys (two independent deciders concurring) for anything that cannot be undone, and keep Tier 3 with the operator regardless of any score. Claiming judgment can be fully eliminated would itself be a catalog error (overconfidence) — the system minimizes its surface and surrounds the remainder.
